DESIGN & EXPERIENCE / 4 MIN READ

Clarity before code.

Why a shared prototype can make your next build easier to understand.

IN THIS ARTICLE

Make assumptions visible.Follow the everyday task.Use the right level of detail.Bring engineering into the conversation.Keep the decisions with the design.

Make assumptions visible.

Different people can agree on a feature name while imagining very different experiences. A prototype turns that ambiguity into screens, actions and outcomes everyone can discuss.

Follow the everyday task.

Start with the journeys people use most often. Show what happens before and after the ideal path: missing information, an interrupted action, an empty account and a recoverable error.

Use the right level of detail.

Early wireframes help decide structure. More detailed prototypes help test wording, hierarchy and interactions. Match the fidelity to the decision you need to make, rather than polishing every screen immediately.

Bring engineering into the conversation.

Design and development should inform each other. Discuss data, permissions, integrations and performance early enough that they can shape the experience.

Keep the decisions with the design.

Record what was agreed, what is still uncertain and why a direction was chosen. That context makes handover more useful than a collection of screens alone.
